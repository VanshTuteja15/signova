What SIGNOVA is

Research prototype. Shows ASL handshapes only. Not a translator or a replacement for interpreters.

SIGNOVA explores whether AI can leave the screen and communicate through physical motion: speech → AI → sign representation → robotic motion. A person speaks, the laptop turns speech into text, a gloss engine picks signs the hand can physically make, and an ESP32 moves the servos.

What it cannot do

  • ASL uses the hands, arms, head, shoulders, torso and face. This prototype has one hand and no arm, so it shows handshapes only: no facial grammar, no movement through space, no pointing (pronouns are skipped).
  • Without a finger-spread joint, U and V look the same and 4 and B share a handshape.
  • All poses are drafts until a fluent signer validates them. Results from signer sessions are reported honestly, including negative feedback.
  • Communication is one-way. It does not understand signing.

Responsible AI

  • The AI can only output signs that are in the library: the sign IDs are an enum in the response schema, and a validator rejects anything else.
  • Audio is processed in memory and not saved unless "Save recordings for testing" is ticked. The header shows which speech engine and whether Claude (cloud, Anthropic API) is in use.
  • Evaluation data stores no names unless a participant label is typed on purpose; CSV files stay on this laptop.
